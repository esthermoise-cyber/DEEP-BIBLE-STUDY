# Walkthrough Video — Narration Script

A ready-to-record script for a ~5-minute screen-recorded tour of the app.
Read the **SAY** lines aloud; follow the **DO** lines on screen.

**How to record (no software needed):**
- **iPhone:** Settings → Control Center → add *Screen Recording*. Swipe down,
  press the record button, press and hold it first to turn the **microphone ON**.
- **Android:** swipe down twice → *Screen Record* → turn the **microphone ON**.
- **Computer:** Windows `Win + G` (Xbox Game Bar) · Mac `Shift + Cmd + 5` ·
  or free: OBS Studio, Loom.

Tip: do one practice run without recording. Speak slower than feels natural.
Pauses are fine — they get trimmed, and they help viewers keep up.

---

## 0:00 — Open

**DO:** App open on the **📖 How to Use** or **Start Here** page.

**SAY:**
> "This is Deep Bible Study — the Truth Project. It's a research tool for
> studying biblical history, ancient Israel, ancient Hebrew, and where today's
> religions and customs actually came from. It has one rule that runs through
> everything: it never claims something is more certain than the evidence
> allows. Let me show you how to use it."

---

## 0:25 — The evidence badges (the heart of the app)

**DO:** On **Start Here**, scroll slowly through the six colored tier cards.

**SAY:**
> "Start here, because these six colors are the whole idea. Every entry in this
> app wears one of these badges. Green means documented — there's physical
> evidence, an inscription, an excavation. Blue means most scholars agree, by
> inference. Orange means genuinely debated — real experts disagree. Purple
> means tradition only — believed, but never verified. And red means the
> evidence contradicts it, no matter how often you've heard it.
>
> So you never have to take this app's word for anything. You look at the
> color, and you know exactly how much weight to give it."

---

## 1:00 — Reading an entry

**DO:** Tap **Origins of Practices**. Open the **Christmas on December 25** entry.
Point out the badge, the body, then scroll to the **Sources** line.

**SAY:**
> "Here's a real entry — Christmas on December 25. Look: the badge says
> *genuinely debated*, and the entry explains why. There are two serious
> scholarly explanations for that date, and it gives you both — then it says
> plainly that the popular claim 'Christmas is just a renamed pagan festival'
> states as fact what is actually one of two live theories. And notice it also
> corrects the *opposite* claim. It cuts both directions.
>
> And at the bottom — sources. Every entry ends with where the information
> came from, so you can go check it yourself."

---

## 1:40 — Search

**DO:** Type **Easter** in the search box. Scroll the results.

**SAY:**
> "The search box searches everything at once. Watch — 'Easter'. It pulls
> results from several different sections, tells me which section each came
> from, and highlights my word. Try anything: Sheol, Kongo, tithing, Enoch."

---

## 2:05 — Contradictions & Context

**DO:** Tap **Contradictions & Context**. Open the first entry. Scroll it slowly.

**SAY:**
> "This section handles the passages people argue about, Old Testament to New.
> But read this first entry before anything else — it's about the rules.
>
> You know how people read a verse literally when they agree with it, and call
> it a metaphor when they don't? This entry sets the rules for that *before*
> you know whether you'll like the answer. Genre first. What the original
> language actually allows. What the first audience assumed. And a consistency
> test: if your method only ever produces conclusions you already held, it
> isn't a method — it's a mirror.
>
> Then every entry after this applies those same rules in the open — the two
> creation accounts, the genealogies of Jesus, Paul versus James — and grades
> each proposed answer honestly. Some tensions dissolve under context. Some
> stay unresolved, and it says so. Then it leaves the conclusion to you."

---

## 2:55 — Learning Hebrew, with sound

**DO:** Tap **Academy** → open **Lesson 2**. Tap a 🔊 button on a letter row —
**let the sound play on the recording.**

**SAY:**
> "You can also learn ancient Hebrew here. Eight short lessons — this one's the
> alphabet. Every letter with its name, its sound, its number value, and the
> picture it started as. Bet was a house. Ayin was an eye.
>
> And you can hear it. [tap 🔊 — pause and let it play]
>
> Pass the little quiz at the end and the lesson gets a green check. By lesson
> eight, you're reading Genesis 1:1 in Hebrew, word by word."

**DO:** Tap **Hebrew Dictionary**. Search *shalom*. Tap **🔊 Hear**.

**SAY:**
> "There's a dictionary too — fifty foundation words, each with how to say it,
> and the honest full range of meaning. Shalom isn't just 'peace' — it's
> wholeness, well-being, completeness. [tap 🔊] That range is the kind of thing
> translations flatten, and it changes how you read whole passages."

---

## 3:45 — The AI

**DO:** Tap **Truth Analyzer**. Show the key box, then the 🎤 button and the
lens dropdown. If your key is saved, ask a real question out loud and let the
answer come back — **this is the strongest moment in the video, don't skip it.**

**SAY:**
> "And there's an AI you can talk to. It's optional — you add your own key from
> Anthropic, and it's stored only on your own device.
>
> Once it's on, I can tap the microphone and just ask out loud, and have it read
> the answer back to me. It remembers the conversation, so I can ask follow-ups.
>
> And this dropdown is my favorite part — interpretive lenses. I can ask the
> same question the way a Rabbinic Jew would frame it, or a Catholic, or a
> Messianic believer, or a secular historian — and it states each one's
> assumptions before it answers. Or I pick 'compare three lenses' and see them
> side by side.
>
> It's under the same rules as everything else: grade every claim, name real
> sources, and when the evidence runs out, say so instead of making something up."

---

## 4:35 — Your own space

**DO:** Tap a ⭐ on any entry → open **⭐ Saved**. Then **📓 Notebook** — add a
note, show the **Export** button.

**SAY:**
> "Anything worth keeping, you star — it goes in your Saved tab. And the
> Notebook is your own research space: write your questions, save any AI answer
> with one tap, and export the whole thing as a file to print or back up. All of
> it stays on your device."

---

## 5:00 — Close

**DO:** Back to **📖 How to Use** or **Start Here**.

**SAY:**
> "That's it. It's free, there are no ads, and it doesn't track you. If you ever
> find an entry that claims more certainty than its evidence allows — tell me,
> because that's a bug.
>
> The whole point is this: the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth
> — and enough evidence in front of you that you can decide for yourself."
