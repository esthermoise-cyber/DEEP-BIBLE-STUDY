## TRACK 1 — Welcome, and why this app is unbiased (~2 min)

Welcome to Deep Bible Study — the Truth Project.

This is a research tool for anyone who sincerely wants to study Scripture, biblical history, ancient Israel, ancient Hebrew, and where today's religions and customs actually came from — without being pushed toward any denomination, any institution, or any political ideology.

It runs on one rule that never bends: never present a claim as more certain than the evidence allows.

So how is this app unbiased? Not by pretending to have no viewpoint — every honest work has one. It is unbiased because of four things you can check for yourself.

First. Every single entry carries an evidence grade. Six colors. Green means documented — there is physical proof: an inscription, an excavation, a coin, a contemporary record. Blue means scholarly consensus — most experts agree, by inference. Orange means genuinely debated — qualified people disagree, and anyone who tells you it is settled is not telling you the whole truth. Purple means tradition only — believed and passed down for centuries, but never verified. And red means the evidence actually contradicts it. And when the evidence simply runs out, this app says so, in those exact words: the available evidence does not allow a reliable conclusion at this time.

Second. It corrects myths in both directions. Popular church traditions get examined honestly — and so do popular claims against religion, many of which are equally false. Easter does not come from Ishtar. The Council of Nicaea did not pick which books go in the Bible. Both of those are myths, and this app says so, no matter which side is disappointed. Truth is not a team sport.

Third. Every entry ends with its sources. Named inscriptions, named manuscripts, named excavations, ancient authors, peer-reviewed studies. Never a vague "historians say." The actual documents, so you can go and check the work yourself.

And fourth. It separates three questions that people constantly blur together. What happened — that is history, and it can be answered. What does the text say in the original language — that is textual, and it can be checked. And what should you believe — that one belongs to you. This app answers the first two, and hands you the third.

## TRACK 2 — How it thinks (~2 min)

So how does this app decide what is true?

It uses the same method professional historians use, and it shows its work, so you can push back on it.

It starts with physical evidence. Archaeology, inscriptions, coins, destruction layers. This is the strongest kind of evidence, because it cannot be rewritten later by whoever won.

Then contemporary written sources — documents written at or near the time of the event, especially by opposing sides. When Assyrian royal records, Babylonian chronicles, and the Bible independently describe the same event, confidence becomes very high.

Then multiple independent attestation. A claim found in several sources that did not copy from each other is far stronger than one found in a single late source. And here is a subtlety that matters enormously: ten people repeating what one person told them is one witness, not ten.

Then the criterion of embarrassment. Details that make the author's own side look bad are unlikely to be invented. Scripture recording Israel's constant idolatry, or a king's humiliating defeat, actually counts in favor of the honesty of those passages.

Then pattern reasoning — used honestly. Where the records are missing, historians reason from patterns: how empires behaved, how languages change, how customs spread. That can point to what is most likely. And this app always labels that as inference. Never as proven fact.

And finally, absence of evidence, weighed honestly. If a large event should have left traces and none are found, that lowers confidence. But absence of evidence is weak evidence of absence — it is not nothing, and it is not proof either. The app says which, plainly, instead of pretending.

Now, one more thing, about reading Scripture itself. You know how people read a verse literally when they agree with it, and call it a metaphor when they don't? This app sets the reading rules before it knows whether you will like the answer. Genre first — law, narrative, poetry, prophecy, and parable each read differently, and the first audience knew that. Then what the Hebrew or the Greek actually allows. Then what that first audience assumed. Then how other Scripture, and the earliest documented readers, took the passage. And then a consistency test: if your method only ever produces the conclusions you already held, it is not a method. It is a mirror.

## TRACK 5 — How to use it (~2 min)

Here is how to actually use it.

The menu at the top is your map. Every subject has its own tab. On a phone, the tabs wrap onto several lines, so scroll to see them all.

The search box searches everything at once. When in doubt, just type there. Try Easter. Try Sheol. Try Kongo, or tithing, or Enoch, or Quirinius.

To read any entry: look at the colored badge first — that tells you exactly how much weight to give what follows. Then read the body, which tells you what is proven and what is not. Then check the sources line at the bottom, and go verify anything you want.

Two small buttons sit on every entry. The star saves it to your Saved tab. And the arrow copies a clean, fully sourced summary that you can paste into a message or a post.

To turn on the artificial intelligence — and this is the only setup step in the entire app — go to platform dot claude dot com, create an account, make an A P I key, and paste it into the Truth Analyzer tab. Your key is stored only on your own device, and sent only to Anthropic. Never to anyone else. Anthropic charges by usage, typical study questions cost only pennies, and you can set a spending cap on their site.

Once it is on, tap the microphone and simply ask out loud. Tick the box that reads answers aloud, and you can have an entire spoken conversation — ask, listen, ask again.

Here is a good first session. Open Start Here and learn the six badges. Then search for something you have always wondered about. Then open Contradictions and Context and read the very first entry, about reading rules. Then star anything that grabs you. And then begin Academy, lesson one.

Everything you save stays on your own device. There are no advertisements, and nothing here tracks you.

And if you ever find an entry that claims more certainty than its evidence allows — that is a bug, and this app wants to know about it.

Because the whole point is this. The truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth — and enough evidence placed in front of you that you can decide for yourself.
